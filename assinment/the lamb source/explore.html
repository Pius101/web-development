Plant Classification

How Families, Genera and Species Connect

Plant classification in this Explorer moves from Family → Genus → Species. Each step narrows the classification from a broad group of related plants to a more specific group, ending with an individual species.

Why this matters

Knowing the relationship between family, genus and species makes it easier to understand how plants are grouped and how one plant is related to another.

Family
A family is a broad taxonomic group containing one or more related genera. Plants placed in the same family share important structural or evolutionary characteristics.
Genus
A genus sits below the family level and brings together species that are more closely related to one another. The genus forms the first part of a scientific species name.
Species
A species is the most specific classification level shown in this website. It refers to a particular kind of plant within a genus.

From broad to specific

One classification level leads to the next

The classification used on this website progresses in one direction: Family → Genus → Species. The family is the broadest of these three levels. Each family contains genera whose members share more characteristics, and each genus then contains individual species. Moving down the hierarchy therefore makes the classification increasingly specific.

  1. Start with the family to identify the large group a plant belongs to.
  2. Move to the genus to narrow that group to more closely related plants.
  3. Finish at the species to identify the specific plant represented in the Explorer.
